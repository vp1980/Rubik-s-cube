# Baseline — Marking Key & Diagnostic Map

For the tutor. Every question is mapped to a ladder code from
`../skill/references/curriculum-ladders-y5.md`, so the first progress log builds
straight from the results rather than from an impression.

**Total: 67 marks** — Part A 20 · Part B 14 · Part C 18 · Part D 8 · Part E 7

**The total is the least useful number on this page.** What matters is the
section profile, the timings, and the wrong-answer patterns in §7. A child
scoring 40/67 with a fluent sprint and weak inference needs a completely
different programme from one scoring 40/67 the other way round.

---

## 1. How to run it

- **Cold.** No revision, no preparation, no advance warning of topics. Say this
  to parents and explain why: a coached baseline produces confident wrong numbers,
  and you will then spend six weeks teaching the wrong things.
- **Part A is strictly timed at 4 minutes.** Stop them at 4:00 exactly. Mark how
  far they got as well as how many were right — the two are different measurements.
- **Everything else is untimed, but record the time taken per part.** Speed is a
  Year 5 target in its own right and it will not show up in a score.
- **Do not help,** but note where they asked. A question a child wanted help on is
  data.
- **Leave the last 10 minutes to teach.** Take the most interesting error they
  made, teach it properly, and get them to a correct answer. The parent needs to
  see teaching, and the child needs to leave the session having succeeded.
- **Do not give the child a mark.** "There were some really interesting ones in
  there" is enough.

## 2. Part A — Number Sprint (20 marks, 4 min)

| Q | Ans | Q | Ans | Q | Ans | Q | Ans |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 56 | 6 | 64 | 11 | 650 | 16 | 12 |
| 2 | 9 | 7 | 9 | 12 | 625 | 17 | 350 |
| 3 | 84 | 8 | 132 | 13 | 100 | 18 | 90 |
| 4 | 54 | 9 | 42 | 14 | 48 | 19 | 11 |
| 5 | 12 | 10 | 9 | 15 | 210 | 20 | 135 |

**This is the single highest-signal item in the paper.** Record two numbers:
**attempted** and **correct**. Band on what was *right* — attempts alone say
nothing, because twenty wrong answers in twenty seconds is not recall.

| Correct in 4 min | Reading |
|---|---|
| 18–20 | Recall is automatic. `M2a AUTO`. Move on. |
| 13–17 | Knows them, computing rather than recalling. `M2a SECURE`. Daily drilling for 2–3 weeks. |
| 8–12 | Not automatic. **`M2a PRAC` — this is now the first priority in the programme**, ahead of new content. |
| Under 8 | Recall is not there yet. `M2a INTRO`. Weeks 1–2 go here and almost nowhere else. |

Then read the gap between the two numbers:

- **Attempted well ahead of correct** (five or more) — answering fast without
  checking. That is a rushing habit, not a recall strength, and it needs a
  checking routine *before* any speed work. Do not mistake it for fluency.
- **Correct close to attempted, but few attempted** — accurate and slow. A (T)
  pattern: the knowledge is there and the speed is not. This is the one that
  costs marks on the day and it is fixed by automaticity, not reteaching.

---|---|
| 18–20, nearly all correct | Recall is automatic. `M2a AUTO`. Move on. |
| 13–17, mostly correct | Knows them, computing rather than recalling. `M2a SECURE`. Daily drilling for 2–3 weeks. |
| 8–12 | Not automatic. **`M2a PRAC` — this is now the first priority in the programme**, ahead of new content. |
| Under 8 | Working everything out from scratch. `M2a INTRO`. Weeks 1–2 go here and almost nowhere else. |

If accuracy is high but attempted is low, the problem is speed, not knowledge —
that is a (T) pattern and it will cost them the real paper.

## 5. Part D — Verbal Reasoning (8 marks)

| Q | Answer | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | b) plentiful | V1 | Vocabulary |
| 2 | d) tulip | V2 | The others are trees |
| 3 | QS | V3 | Both letters advance by 4 each time |
| 4 | UPSDI | V4 | Each letter moves forward one |
| 5 | NEVER | V5 | Hidden across "pla**never**y" |
| 6 | hive | V6 | Creature → its home |
| 7 | Ana | V7 | Ben last; Ana before Sam; Tom after Sam → Ana, Sam, Tom, Ben |
| 8 | 14 | V8 | (4 × 5) − (2 × 3) |

Score Q1, Q5 separately in your head from Q3, Q4, Q7: the first group is
**vocabulary** and improves slowly with reading; the second is **technique** and
improves fast with drilling. A child who gets the technique items and misses the
vocabulary ones needs a reading programme, not VR practice — and vice versa.

## 6. Part E — Non-Verbal Reasoning (7 marks)

| Q | Answer | Code | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | B | N1 | Sides increase by one; shading alternates → 7 sides, unshaded |
| 2 | E | N2 | In every other figure the number of dots equals the number of sides |
| 3 | B | N3 | The outer shape and inner circle swap sizes |
| 4 | A | N4 | Rows fix the shape, columns fix the shading (none → half → full) |
| 5 | A | N5 | Reflection, not rotation. B is the original; C is flipped top-to-bottom; D is turned through 180° |
| 6 | E | N6 | In a row of four faces the opposite pairs are the 1st/3rd and 2nd/4th — so B–D and C–E (and A–F) |
| 7 | 5 | N8 | Four are visible; a fifth must sit beneath the raised cube or it would float |

Q5 and Q7 carry the most diagnostic weight. Choosing **D** on Q5 means rotating
instead of reflecting — extremely common and completely fixable. Answering **4**
on Q7 means counting only what can be seen, which is the core skill the question
tests.

## 7. Common wrong answers and what they mean

This is the part worth reading. Each of these is a specific, named
misconception with a specific response — not "needs more practice".

| Where | Wrong answer | What it means | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 47,400 or 47,000 with hesitation | Rounding to the wrong place | 5 min on place-value columns |
| B2 | −17 or 17 | Treating the rise as a fall, or ignoring the sign | Number line, explicitly |
| B4 | 32 | Left-to-right, no BIDMAS | Teach BIDMAS as a rule; very quick win |
| B6 | 4/12 | Adding numerators and denominators | Classic (K). Reteach common denominators from equivalence |
| B8 | 35/100 | Correct but not simplified | (M), not (K). Read the command word |
| B9 | 15:85 or 16:15 | Treating time as decimal | Very common. Teach 60-minute bridging |
| B11 | £22.50 each | Ignored the ratio, split in half | Reteach ratio from "how many equal parts?" |
| B13 | £12.80 | Bought 3 pencils rather than 3 packs | (R) misread, not a maths gap. Underline command words |
| C3 | "make it pretty" | Literal reading of a figurative word | The centre of E3. Expect this; it is the main teaching target |
| C5 | "He was being modest" | Missed the negation — "not modesty" | (R). Watch whether it recurs |
| C6 | Only one piece of evidence | Did not read "give two" | (R), costs real marks on the day |
| C8 | Retells the plot | Cannot summarise a theme | E9 gap. Very common and very trainable |
| C12 | "Corrects" *occasion* | Not checking, pattern-matching the instruction | Interesting. Note it — suggests answering the expected question rather than the actual one |
| C13 | keeper's | Singular possessive for a plural noun | One rule, 5 minutes |
| D3 | QR or PR | Found the pattern in one letter only | Teach: check both strands |
| D7 | Sam or Tom | Did not use "Ben is last" | Teach the grid method for V7 |
| E5 | D | Rotated instead of reflecting | The single most common NVR error. Explicit teaching, fixes fast |
| E7 | 4 | Counted only visible cubes | The point of the question. Teach "what holds it up?" |

## 8. Turning the results into a log

Convert each section to a starting level. These bands are **deliberately
cautious** — it is much cheaper to advance a child in week 3 than to discover in
week 8 that you pitched too high.

| Section score | Starting level for topics in that section |
|---|---|
| 90%+ | `SECURE` — verify speed before logging `AUTO` |
| 70–89% | `PRAC` |
| 50–69% | `INTRO` |
| Under 50% | `—` for the untouched topics; `INTRO` for anything attempted sensibly |

Then:

1. Fill in `../skill/assets/pupil-log-template.md` per pupil, topic by topic
   using the codes above — not one level for "maths".
2. Enter the Part A result into the **timing benchmarks** table. That is the
   baseline you will report progress against in March, and it is the number
   parents find most convincing.
3. Set the **priority queue**. For nearly every Year 5 pupil starting cold, if
   the sprint came back under 13 attempted, the answer is times tables, and
   everything else waits two weeks.
4. Write the starting-point summary — **not** a full fortnightly report. One data
   point is not a trend. The first real report comes two sessions later.

Tell the family plainly: the first two or three weeks remain calibration and
these levels will move. And say before handing back anything numerical that the
September figure exists to be beaten — some parents will read a low baseline as
a prediction, and that is a conversation worth having early rather than late.
