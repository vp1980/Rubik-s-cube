# Curriculum Ladders — Year 5

The topic sequence for each strand, what mastery means at each rung, and the
40-week plan from September to the exam. Use these to decide what comes next and
to find the prerequisite when something has to be dropped back.

## 1. Mastery scale

Every topic in the log carries one of these:

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `—` | Not yet introduced |
| `INTRO` | Met once, needs heavy support |
| `PRAC` | Can do it with prompting; errors common |
| `SECURE` | Independent and accurate; occasional slips |
| `AUTO` | Fast **and** accurate under a mixed, timed set |
| `RETEACH` | Was higher, has slipped — priority for next session |

**At Year 5 the target is `AUTO`, not `SECURE`.** This is the single most
important difference from a younger child's programme. In the exam a merely
`SECURE` topic costs time, and running out of time is what actually separates
children who pass from children who know the material. A pupil who can do
long division correctly in ninety seconds will not finish the paper.

So: a topic is not logged as `AUTO` on accuracy alone. It needs accuracy **at
speed, in a set mixing several topics**, because recognising which method to
apply is a separate skill from applying it.

## 2. Maths ladder

**M1 Place value** — read, write, order, compare and round numbers to
1,000,000; negative numbers; Roman numerals. *Assumed secure by Year 5 — check
it in the baseline and reteach immediately if not, because everything below
depends on it.*

**M2 Four operations** — column methods with exchange; long multiplication;
short and long division with remainders; order of operations (BIDMAS).

**M2a Times tables to 12×12 — instant recall.** *The single highest-leverage
item in the whole programme. If recall is not automatic, fix this before
anything else, in parallel with whatever else is running. Every topic from M3
onward is slower and more error-prone without it, and the cost compounds over
forty weeks.*

**M3 Fractions** — equivalence, simplifying, comparing, fractions of amounts,
four operations with fractions, mixed numbers and improper fractions.

**M4 Decimals & percentages** — place value to 3dp, converting between
fractions/decimals/percentages, percentage of amount, percentage increase and
decrease.

**M5 Measure & time** — metric and imperial conversions, perimeter, area
(including compound shapes), volume, 12/24-hour time, timetables, elapsed time,
speed–distance–time.

**M6 Geometry** — 2D and 3D properties, angles (on a line, around a point, in
triangles and quadrilaterals), symmetry, coordinates in four quadrants,
translation, reflection, rotation.

**M7 Data** — bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, two-way tables, mean, median,
mode and range; reading and interpreting, not just drawing.

**M8 Ratio & proportion** — sharing in a ratio, unequal sharing, scaling
recipes, direct proportion, scale drawings and maps.

**M9 Algebra** — missing number problems, function machines, simple formulae,
substitution, sequences and nth term, solving one- and two-step equations.

**M10 Multi-step problem solving** — two or more operations, working backwards,
logic and number puzzles, word problems with irrelevant information. *This is
where 11+ papers actually live. Everything above is scaffolding for it, and it
needs deliberate practice in its own right — children who are secure on M1–M9
still fail M10 questions if they have only ever met topics in isolation.*

## 3. English ladder

**E1 Reading fluency** — reads aloud with expression, does not stumble on
unfamiliar words. Assess by listening, not by testing.

**E2 Literal comprehension** — retrieves stated facts; can quote the words that
answer the question.

**E3 Vocabulary in context** — works out unfamiliar words from surrounding text.
*The highest-value English skill for the 11+ and the slowest to build. Ten new
words a week at Year 5, every week, no exceptions, with spot-checks on words
introduced a fortnight earlier. This is also the engine of the entire verbal
reasoning strand — V1 synonyms and antonyms is vocabulary wearing a different
hat, and no amount of VR technique substitutes for not knowing the words.*

**E4 Inference** — "how do we know", "what does this suggest", character motive
and mood, reading between the lines. *The step most children stall on, and the
one that most reliably separates scores at this level.*

**E5 Author's craft** — similes, metaphors, personification, alliteration,
onomatopoeia; and crucially **why** the writer chose a word or device, not just
naming it.

**E6 Spelling patterns** — prefixes, suffixes, homophones, plurals, silent
letters, common exception words, root words and etymology.

**E7 Punctuation** — commas in lists and to mark clauses, apostrophes
(possession vs omission), speech punctuation, brackets and dashes, colons and
semicolons.

**E8 Grammar terminology** — word classes, tenses, main and subordinate clauses,
subject and object, active and passive, modal verbs, subjunctive. *Named grammar
is heavily tested by GL papers — confirm the board before deciding how much
weight this gets.*

**E9 Summarising & sequencing** — reduce a paragraph to one sentence, order
events, identify the main idea.

**E10 Extended writing** — planning, paragraphing, openers, ambitious
vocabulary, editing under time. *Tested by some boards and not others. Confirm
before investing here — it is expensive in session time.*

## 4. Verbal reasoning ladder

- **V1 Synonyms and antonyms** — *this is vocabulary. It lives or dies on E3 and on reading volume.*
- **V2 Odd one out / word categories**
- **V3 Letter sequences and alphabet positions**
- **V4 Word codes and substitution ciphers**
- **V5 Hidden words and compound words**
- **V6 Analogies**
- **V7 Logic and deduction puzzles** — grid puzzles, ordering from constraints
- **V8 Number-word problems** — arithmetic dressed as verbal reasoning
- **V9 Complete the sentence / missing word**
- **V10 Word formation and letter-move puzzles**

VR splits cleanly into two halves and they need different treatment. V1, V5, V9
are vocabulary — they improve with reading and word banks, slowly, over months.
V3, V4, V7, V10 are technique — they improve fast with drilling because each has
a reliable method. **Drill the technique items; feed the vocabulary items.**
Confusing the two is why children plateau.

## 5. Non-verbal reasoning ladder

- **N1 Series and sequences**
- **N2 Odd one out**
- **N3 Analogies** — A is to B as C is to ?
- **N4 Matrices** — 2×2 and 3×3
- **N5 Rotation and reflection**
- **N6 Nets and 3D visualisation**
- **N7 Codes and symbol logic**
- **N8 Spatial reasoning and cube counting**

**NVR is the most trainable strand in the 11+.** It responds strongly to roughly
eight weeks of focused practice and then flattens. The implication for planning
is specific: do not spread it thinly across forty weeks, and do not leave it to
the end. Run a concentrated NVR block (see the plan below), get it to `AUTO`,
then maintain it with a few questions a week rather than continuing to teach it.

The method to teach explicitly, because it is what separates fast from slow:
scan one attribute at a time — shape, then shading, then rotation, then count,
then position — rather than staring at the whole figure hoping to see it.

## 6. The 40-week plan

From September of Year 5 to the exam at the start of Year 6. Adjust per pupil
from the baseline — a pupil who is already `SECURE` on a block skips it and the
plan compresses in their favour. **Check each pupil's position against this plan
fortnightly and report it.** A gap that is noticed in November is recoverable; the
same gap noticed in May is not.

| Weeks | Maths | English | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | Baseline, then M2a times tables to automaticity — everything else waits on this | Baseline; start the vocabulary engine (10/week from week 1) | Baseline only |
| **3–8** | M1–M2 secure, M3 fractions | E2, E3, E4 — inference is the priority | V1–V3, light |
| **9–14** | M4 decimals & percentages | E4 continued, E5 author's craft | V4–V7 technique drilling |
| **15–20** | M5 measure, M6 geometry | E6, E7, E8 grammar and punctuation | **NVR block — N1–N5, concentrated** |
| **21–26** | M7 data, M8 ratio | E9 summarising; E10 if the board tests it | **NVR block — N6–N8**; VR maintenance |
| **27–32** | M9 algebra, M10 multi-step | Mixed timed comprehension | Mixed timed VR and NVR |
| **33–36** | M10 and mixed timed papers | Mixed timed papers | Mixed timed papers |
| **37–40** | **Rehearsal only** — full papers, mock conditions, marking analysis | Rehearsal only | Rehearsal only |

Three things about this plan matter more than the topic order:

**Content coverage ends at week 36 — around Easter.** Weeks 37–40 are stamina
and technique, not learning. If a topic is still being taught in the final
block, it will not be secure enough to survive exam conditions anyway, and the
time is better spent making everything else faster.

**Weeks 1–2 look wasteful and are not.** Times-table automaticity is the
cheapest possible investment. Two weeks spent there returns time in every
maths session for the remaining thirty-eight.

**The NVR block is deliberately late and deliberately concentrated.** Running it
in weeks 15–26 means it peaks close enough to the exam to still be sharp, and
concentrating it exploits the fact that NVR improves fast and then plateaus.

## 7. Board differences

Confirm the target schools and board for each pupil before week 3. The ladder
above is board-agnostic; these are the adjustments once it is known.

**GL Assessment** — discrete VR and NVR papers with a published list of question
types, defined maths and English syllabus. The most coachable board: drill the
question types directly. Named grammar terminology (E8) is heavily tested.

**CEM / Durham-style** — vocabulary-heavy, mixed and tightly timed sections,
deliberately harder to coach. Weight E3 vocabulary and reading volume far above
technique drilling, and practise switching between question types under time.

**ISEB pre-test** — sat in **January of Year 6**, not September. This shortens
the runway by roughly eight weeks and the plan above must be compressed
accordingly, mostly by cutting the rehearsal block. Adaptive and computer-based,
so practise on screen, not only on paper.

Preparing for the wrong shape wastes a term, and the cost is not recoverable.

## 8. Daily non-negotiables

Outside the three weekly sessions, these do more work than any worksheet:

- **20 minutes of reading**, every day, fiction at or slightly above comfort
  level. This is the single biggest driver of E3, E4 and the vocabulary half of
  VR, and nothing else substitutes for it.
- **Times tables** until instant, then twice a week to maintain.
- **Ten new words a week**, with the fortnight-later spot-check. Words that fail
  the check carry forward.

Tell parents these three explicitly and early. They are free, they run without
the tutor, and a family doing them is worth more than a family doing extra
worksheets.
